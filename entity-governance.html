The Problem

Structures that grew instead of being designed

Most group structures were never designed — they accumulated. An acquisition here, a market entry there, a dormant company nobody got around to closing. Each entity carries statutory filings, audits, tax returns, directors' duties and banking relationships, whether it earns money or not.

The bill arrives at the worst moments: in due diligence, when a buyer's lawyers find unfiled accounts in a dormant subsidiary. In an audit, when intercompany positions don't reconcile. In a restructuring, when nobody can say quickly what each entity actually does.

Exit-readiness is a continuous state, not a project you start once a sale is on the table.
