Case Study
Listed Multinational · Cross-Border M&A

Integrating a factory acquisition into an existing market

The group acquired a production facility abroad, in a market where it already had a commercial presence. The integration target was that single division — one entity, one acquisition — and the prize was the facility's licenses and platform.

What we did

Led the finance integration of the acquired entity in close coordination with the IMO: Day-1 readiness, reporting alignment and entity governance integration — with the integration backlog kept strictly separate from the transformation that would repurpose the site.

Outcome

The acquired entity reported to group standards on deadline; the transformation that converted the site to the group's own production ran afterwards as a scoped, costed programme rather than scope creep.

Case Study
Manufacturing Group · M&A

A greenfield-market acquisition that reached far deeper

In a separate deal, the group acquired a factory in a country where it had no existing market presence. With nothing local to build on, the integration scope ran much deeper and broader — and the transformation was central, not a follow-on.

What we did

Stood up the acquired entity's finance and governance from the ground up, then led the transformation that took the site from production-only to production and packing — expanding what the facility could do for the group.

Outcome

A fully integrated entity reporting to group standards, and a transformed operation delivering more of the value chain in-house.

Case Study
International Group · Governance

A governance framework built from the ground up

Branch and entity governance had never been formally designed — and without that foundation, no transformation initiative could get traction. Decisions that needed legal, HR and IT stalled because nothing owned them.

What we did

Defined a branch and entity governance framework from the ground up, built on cross-functional ownership with finance in focus — since that's where the impacts land heaviest. Every workstream co-owned by finance and the function it affected, with decision rights agreed before design work resumed.

Outcome

A governance framework the organisation could actually run on; blocked decisions cleared within two cycles, and later initiatives had a foundation to build on instead of stalling.
